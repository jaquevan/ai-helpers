# Journey map

Start from `canvas.json` in this folder. People expect one actor's path, read left to right, with phases as columns and the same lanes in every column.

This follows the five parts of a journey map: actor, scenario and expectations, phases, actions with mindsets and emotions, and opportunities. Pain points sit just above opportunities.

## How to fill it

- **One actor.** The map is one point of view. A second persona is a second canvas.
- **Scenario and expectations** say what situation this is and what the actor expects to happen. Mark the map current-state or future-state in the scenario node.
- **Phases are columns**, left to right. Rename Discover, Evaluate, Use, and Support to this scenario's real stages. Add or remove a whole column, and keep every lane in the columns you keep. Do not switch to a top-to-bottom flow.
- **Actions** are the narrative of that phase, not every click.
- **Mindsets** are thoughts, questions, and motivations. Use the actor's words when the source has them.
- **Emotions** are one short high or low per phase so the row reads as an arc. Do not write a paragraph there.
- **Pain points** and **opportunities** are the takeaways under the experience. An opportunity says what should change. Add who owns it, or how you would know it worked, only when the source says so.
- An **experience map** uses this same frame. The actor is a generic person, and the scenario is not tied to one product.
- Label assumptions. Do not present invented observations as research.
- Replace every placeholder. Do not leave "Replace with" in the finished canvas.

Lane color is a backup cue. The lane label on the left is what names the row. Do not rely on color alone.
